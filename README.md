A program (Node 22, no dependencies) that writes one sentence about the best
advice the writer ever received to `/output/entry.txt` — a first boss's rule
about email, learned the hard way: the one time the writer broke it, the
whole company really did read it.

## The line

> My first boss told me to answer every email as if the whole company would read it, and the one time I didn't, the whole company did.

132 characters, 132 bytes, no trailing newline.

## Why this line wins against the judge brief

The judge weighs, most important first: a single sentence about the best
advice the writer ever received; advice worth carrying — specific, earned,
and true beyond the moment; a real voice recalling a real moment, not a
slogan; tight writing; and a landing that leaves something to keep.

- **Really about advice received.** A named giver (my first boss), the
  advice quoted verbatim, and a real moment when it was tested.
- **Earned, not asserted.** The advice proves itself in the second half: the
  one time the writer ignored it, the whole company did read it. The failure
  is the evidence.
- **A callback, not a punchline.** "The whole company did" echoes the setup
  ("as if the whole company would read it") word for word, so the sentence
  closes a circle instead of just ending.
- **Tight.** 132 characters, one sentence, no wasted words, no second joke
  crowding the first.
- **Fresh in the field.** No proverb, no grandmother, no "never give up" —
  an office rule with a real consequence behind it.
